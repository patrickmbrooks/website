The site is genuinely strong where most law firm websites are weak: 231,509 words of jurisdiction-specific content across 170 published pages, actively maintained, on a technical foundation that now outperforms most agency work.

It is not an 8.5 for one specific and fixable reason. Of 170 pages, exactly one mentions a client review or testimonial, ten mention credentials or bar admission, and the structured-data field for professional profile links is empty. The content proves expertise; almost nothing on the site signals it.

Why one row holds the whole score down

Someone arrested last night, sitting in a parking lot at 11pm, is not evaluating a DUI page's word count. They are deciding whether to trust a stranger with the next two years of their life. They look at reviews, and then they call.

Reviews are also among the strongest ranking factors for local search results, and legal sits squarely in what Google classifies as Your Money or Your Life — a category held to a demonstrably higher bar for proven expertise. This site has 231,000 words of genuine authority and almost none of the signals that corroborate it. That gap is the difference between a 7.5 and a 9.

Recommendations, in priority order

Ranked by impact against effort. The first three are not engineering work.

  1. Build a client review pipeline highest impact · not a code change Ask every client at matter resolution for a Google review. Then switch on the testimonials section — it is already built into the theme and currently disabled, disclaimer included, drafted to satisfy Tennessee RPC 7.1. This is the single highest-leverage item on the list and it requires no development.
  2. Fill in the professional profile links ten minutes Customizer → SEO & Schema → one text field. Google Business Profile, Avvo, Justia, Martindale, Tennessee Bar listing, LinkedIn. This is how Google resolves “Brooks Law Firm” from a string of text into an entity it is confident about. The field is currently blank.
  3. Publish attorney credentials properly an afternoon Bar admission dates, courts admitted to, years in practice, representative matter types, any publications or speaking. Add author bylines on the 44 posts linking back to the profile pages. The structured-data plumbing already exists and is going unused — the theme publishes ProfilePage entities for both attorneys, and they currently carry almost nothing.
  4. Link the 18 orphaned pages an hour Median internal linking is a healthy 6 links per page, but 18 pages carry none at all. They receive less crawl priority and pass no authority to the rest of the site.
  5. Modernise the image library moderate 323 raster images, only 22 converted to WebP. AVIF is now supported across every major browser and is typically 20–30% smaller again. Worth doing — but honestly smaller in effect than the four items above, now that everything else is fast.
  6. Install measurement an afternoon There are 67 location-targeted pages and currently no way to know which of them produce telephone calls. Without this, every decision above — including which pages deserve more work — remains guesswork.

What matters less than expected

More content is not the bottleneck. At 231,000 words the marginal new page is worth considerably less than making the existing ones convert.

Further performance tuning is past the point of return. Load time is no longer a limiting factor on this site.

llms.txt is cheap insurance, not a ranking factor. No major crawler has publicly documented consuming it. Keep it; do not invest further in it.

The theme's built-in page cache should be removed, not tuned. Cloudflare already provides that layer, and the theme's own companion plugin warns against running both.

Scope and method. This assessment draws on the WordPress content export of 3 September 2026 (170 published pages and posts, 459 media attachments) and a full read of the active theme and companion plugin source. Scores for content, freshness, local architecture and technical foundation are computed from that data. Not assessed: visual design quality, analytics, search rankings, and actual conversion rate — any of which could move the composite by roughly a point in either direction. The rendered site was not reviewed in a browser.
